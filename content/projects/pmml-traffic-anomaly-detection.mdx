## Highlights
- Compared a Poisson GLM with a GRU (PyTorch) on 5 years of hourly Toronto traffic counts, training on 2020-2024 and testing on held-out 2025; the GRU cut RMSE by 54%.
- Scored anomalies with KL divergence; the interpretable GLM detected them better (F1 0.49 vs 0.14).

Solo course paper for Probabilistic Models & Machine Learning at York University.

## Focus
This project was less about chasing one model score and more about understanding what different anomaly detectors assume.

## What I compared
- A Poisson GLM for interpretable modeling of traffic count behavior.
- A GRU sequence model for temporal dependency capture.
- KL-divergence anomaly scoring to inspect shifts between expected and observed behavior.

## Main lesson
The better forecaster was not the better detector. If the anomaly score is poorly calibrated, a more complex model can still produce noisy operational signals, so evaluation had to stay tied to practical alert usefulness, not just loss curves.
